# 《Criminal Investigation》技能包（14 个）

## 1. Evidence and Proof
- R: Criminal investigation and evidence are inseparable; evidence is used to establish proof.
- I: From “who did it” to “what evidence supports or excludes each explanation”.
- A1: The book emphasizes the role, strengths, and limitations of all major forms of evidence.
- E: Start with evidence, then build proof, then evaluate alternative hypotheses.
- B: Evidence proves source or fact, not automatically behavior or intent.

## 2. Reactive and Proactive Investigations
- R: Investigations can be reactive to a reported crime or proactive against anticipated crime.
- I: From “crime happened, now investigate” to “are we responding or preventing”.
- A1: Different investigation types require different data, resources, and legal authority.
- E: Classify investigation type before choosing methods.
- B: Proactive investigation raises surveillance and civil liberty risks.

## 3. Investigation as Battle, Game, and Puzzle
- R: Investigation can be seen as a battle against offenders, a game of strategy, or a puzzle of evidence.
- I: From “one true story” to “what framework best fits this case”.
- A1: Battle emphasizes adversarial dynamics; puzzle emphasizes inference.
- E: Use multiple perspectives, but choose by problem structure.
- B: Metaphors can oversimplify complex cases.

## 4. Mental Mistakes
- R: Investigators can make predictable mental errors, not just procedural mistakes.
- I: From “evidence says” to “what confirmation, tunnel vision, or bias may be shaping interpretation”.
- A1: The book discusses mental mistakes as key investigation risks.
- E: Deliberately seek disconfirming evidence.
- B: Awareness of bias does not eliminate it.

## 5. Documentation and Report Writing
- R: Documentation is evidence about evidence; poor reports weaken the whole case.
- I: From “I remember” to “what was seen, when, by whom, and how recorded”.
- A1: Chapter 3 discusses documenting evidence through report writing.
- E: Record chain of custody, context, and changes.
- B: A correct conclusion can be undermined by bad documentation.

## 6. Physical Evidence
- R: Physical evidence can connect people, places, objects, and time, but has limitations.
- I: From “DNA matches” to “what physical evidence means in the whole context”.
- A1: Physical evidence is one of the “big three” evidence types.
- E: Analyze collection, preservation, testing, and interpretation.
- B: Physical evidence proves presence, not always action.

## 7. Law of Search and Seizure
- R: Legal authority, warrants, and reasonableness govern how evidence can be obtained.
- I: From “we need the evidence” to “does the method comply with legal rights”.
- A1: Fourth Amendment law constrains searches and seizures.
- E: Before evidence use, check legal authorization and procedure.
- B: Evidence obtained illegally may be excluded.

## 8. Interviews and Eyewitness Identification
- R: Witness memory is fragile; interview and identification methods directly affect accuracy.
- I: From “witness saw him” to “what method was used, how long ago, and what biases were present”.
- A1: The book emphasizes research on human memory and eyewitness identifications.
- E: Use sequential, unbiased procedures and record confidence carefully.
- B: Confident witnesses can still be wrong.

## 9. Confessions
- R: Confessions are powerful but can be false under pressure, deception, or fatigue.
- I: From “he admitted it” to “what interrogation method produced the confession and what corroborates it”.
- A1: Traditional interrogation controversies are discussed.
- E: Treat confessions as evidence requiring corroboration.
- B: False confessions occur and can corrupt cases.

## 10. Behavioral Evidence and Crime Analysis
- R: Behavior, patterns, and crime analysis can narrow suspects but cannot replace physical evidence.
- I: From “profile says” to “what behavior is observable and what alternative patterns exist”.
- A1: Behavioral evidence and psychological profiling support investigation.
- E: Use behavioral evidence as a search tool, not proof.
- B: Profiling has limits and should be tested.

## 11. Digital Devices and Social Media
- R: Digital and social media evidence can reconstruct communication, movement, and relationships.
- I: From “data exists” to “is the data authentic, complete, and lawfully obtained”.
- A1: Chapter 9 covers public, social media, digital devices, and electronic databases.
- E: Verify source, timestamp, chain of custody, and legal basis.
- B: Digital data can be manipulated or misinterpreted.

## 12. Crime-Specific Investigations
- R: Homicide, sexual assault, fraud, and computer crimes each require specialized knowledge.
- I: From “generic case” to “what legal elements, evidence types, and victim dynamics apply”.
- A1: Chapters 10-14 focus on specific crime types.
- E: Adapt investigation framework to offense and legal context.
- B: Specialization is needed but not an excuse for weak basics.

## 13. Testimony and Court Presentation
- R: Investigation only succeeds when evidence can be presented clearly in court.
- I: From “we know the truth” to “can we explain and defend it under challenge”.
- A1: Chapter 15 covers presentation of evidence through testimony.
- E: Prepare evidence, chain of custody, and explanation for court.
- B: Convincing presentation does not replace legal sufficiency.

## 14. Technology and Future
- R: Technology changes investigation, but ethics, law, and human judgment remain central.
- I: From “new tech solves everything” to “what are its legal, privacy, bias, and verification limits”.
- A1: Terrorism, technology, and the future of investigation are discussed.
- E: Evaluate tools by accuracy, legality, transparency, and accountability.
- B: AI and surveillance should not replace independent proof.

## Boundary
- This is a textbook in English; legal rules are U.S.-oriented and vary by jurisdiction.
- It does not replace law enforcement, forensic, or legal professionals.
